Education First Utah & Utah Behavior Services: Same Executives, Two Different Names

An "education advocacy" PAC's own founding paperwork traces to three executives of a private prison company. A separate autism-therapy company's political giving arm has no documented tie to that company — but it sent an identical $2,500 check to the same sitting MIDA Vice Chair, one week after the PAC did.

New here? Education First Utah is a PAC/nonprofit DBA that gives to legislative candidates under an "education advocacy" banner. Utah Behavior Services is a Taylorsville-based Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) therapy company for children with autism that separately makes its own direct political contributions. They look unrelated on the surface — but their giving overlaps on the same recipient, in the same week, and Education First Utah's own founding paperwork traces to executives at a private prison company.

1. What Are These Two Entities?

Education First Utah

DBA #12427437-0151, filed 8/9/2021

Applicant/owner is "Education First, A Utah Nonprofit Corporation" (Entity #10572869-0140). Registered address 500 N Marketplace Drive #100, Centerville — the headquarters of Management & Training Corporation (MTC), a private prison and job-corps contractor. Its own site says it was "organized in 2011" and presents itself as a bipartisan, consensus-driven education-policy advocate with "no litmus test on any issue."

Utah Behavior Services

6013 S Redwood Rd, Taylorsville

A real Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) therapy provider for children with autism spectrum disorder, with multiple Salt Lake County clinic locations. Kerry Gibson's own 2023 lobbyist registration lists it as a client alongside Bridgeway Integrated Healthcare Services — confirmed further by a 2026 contribution filed jointly under both names.

Three separate MTC executives are tied directly to Education First Utah's founding paperwork — not an inferred connection, a documented one. The DBA's own registration filing names Michael P. Petrogeorge as the filer (confirmed VP & General Counsel, MTC), Ammaron Wolfgramm as registered agent (confirmed Director, Treasury & Capital Management, MTC), and MTC's own President, Bob Marquardt, sits on Education First Utah's Executive Committee per its own published leadership page.

"Education First, A Utah Nonprofit Corporation" — the DBA's own applicant/owner — has no findable federal tax-exempt record. Checked directly against four separate sources: the IRS's own Tax Exempt Organization Search, Candid/GuideStar's nonprofit database, ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer, and the Utah Nonprofits Association's member directory. All four come back empty for this entity, while each of those databases readily surfaces other small Utah nonprofits, including several also based in Centerville. Incorporating as a "nonprofit corporation" under Utah state law is a state-law governance structure — it doesn't by itself confer federal 501(c) tax-exempt status, which requires a separate IRS application and determination. Nothing here confirms one way or the other why no record turns up — a never-filed application, a pending one, or registration under a name or EIN not yet tried are all possible — but the absence is real and consistent across every database checked.

5. Who Runs Bridgeway / Utah Behavior Services Now

Utah Behavior Services was co-founded in 2008 by Natalie Whatcott and Sarah Sanders; the corporation itself, "Bridgeway Integrated Healthcare Services, Inc.," was formed in 2010 and adopted "Bridgeway" as its public name in 2025 — its own site states it plainly: "Formerly known as Utah Behavior Services." The current officer registry looks different from that founding story.

Kerry Gibson appearing as the company's own in-house Government Affairs Director — not an outside lobbyist with them as one client among several — is worth noting alongside his other roles documented elsewhere in this project's research (Xalisco LLC, the Sawyer vendor network, the Nadolski/Ogden ties).

Employment litigation, on the public record

Utah Investigative Journalism Project and the Salt Lake Tribune (Taylor Stevens, Nov. 2022) reported that Utah Behavior Services has pursued noncompete enforcement against numerous former employees over several years, including a case against a competing clinic that hired some of them. Parents quoted in the reporting said they worried the disputes could disrupt their children's care; the company's public response to the coverage called it "vicious and personally motivated." A federal court record from 2017 separately confirms Whatcott and Sanders were both "Co-CEOs" as of 2014, in a dispute connected to the company's expansion into St. George — the same market where Sanders later incorporated her own similarly-named company.

The 8 who received money: Logan Monson ($5,000, EFU), Jake Sawyer ($4,500, EFU+UBS), Dave Shallenberger ($2,936, EFU), Mike Schultz ($3,000, UBS), Jefferson Moss ($1,500–$4,000, UBS + likely EFU), Christine Watkins ($500, UBS), Derrin Owens ($2,000, UBS), Don Ipson ($500, UBS).

Committee membership alone doesn't predict funding either. Three sitting Senate Education Committee members on the endorsement slate — Lincoln Fillmore, David Hinkins, and Michael McKell — received zero dollars from either entity, same as everyone else on the endorsed-only list. So the real dividing line isn't "relevant committee" — it's something narrower: a leadership role (Monson is Vice Chair), an existing multi-year relationship, or a specific piece of legislation. An endorsement looks cheap to hand out broadly; an actual check is a separately-targeted decision.

Two names outside the 8/42 pattern are worth a closer look for the opposite reason — a values-fit or funding relationship with no clean structural explanation. Stacy Bernal (SD-3 candidate, Democrat) is a sitting elected member of the Ogden School Board, founder of Awesome Autistic Ogden, and partnered with Ogden PD on an autism safety registry — an actual education policymaker with the clearest values-fit of anyone on the slate, and still endorsed-only, no dollars. Bob Stevenson (HD-16 candidate, $7,500 from EFU — tied with Jerry Stevenson for the largest combined individual total identified anywhere in this dataset) isn't on the 42-name endorsement slate at all — he's funded without a public endorsement, the reverse of Bernal's situation. If values-fit predicted funding, Bernal would be funded; if funding predicted endorsement, Stevenson would be on the list. Neither holds.

7. Testing the Claim

Education First Utah's own site states its standard plainly: "Education First Utah supports candidates for the Utah legislature who are open minded, smart and reasonable in their approach to decision making. Education First Utah does not have a litmus test on any issue for candidates to gain our support. Incumbents with a history of supporting educators and students in their past voting are appreciated." — educationfirstutah.org

That claim is specifically about incumbents' past voting — so it's only fair to test it against sitting legislators who have an actual voting record, not against genuinely new candidates. Of the 42 names on the slate, three have never cast a state legislative vote at all: Stacy Bernal (SD-3, currently Ogden School Board only), Linda Hanks (HD-66), and Mike Bird (HD-35). The claim doesn't apply to them one way or the other. That leaves 32 sitting incumbents with a real record to check against the Utah Education Association's own 2025 legislative scorecard — the most direct, independently-sourced measure of "supporting educators and students in past voting" available.

51%Average UEA score across all 32 endorsed incumbents
40%Average UEA score among the 8 who are actually FUNDED

The four highest-scoring incumbents on the whole slate — Ray Ward, Tracy Miller, Paul Cutler, and David Hinkins, all at 83% — receive no money. Two of the lowest-scoring incumbents on the whole slate — Jake Sawyer and Jefferson Moss, both at 20% — do. If a legislator's actual record of supporting educators explained who gets paid, this would run the other way.

The people EFU actually pays score lower, on average, than the people it merely endorses. Jake Sawyer and Jefferson Moss — two of the 8 funded recipients — tie for the lowest UEA score (20%) of anyone on the entire slate, matched only by two unfunded names (Walt Brooks, Lisa Shephard). Meanwhile several of the highest-scored incumbents on the whole list — Ray Ward (83%), Tracy Miller (83%), Paul Cutler (83%), David Hinkins (83%) — are endorsed and receive nothing. If "history of supporting educators" were the operative standard for money, the ranking would run the other way.

One caveat worth stating plainly: UEA is itself an advocacy organization — a teachers' union — and "UEA's position" reflects its own policy views, not a neutral referee's. On the six bills tracked in 2025, UEA's positions happened to align closely with the Democratic caucus, who score almost uniformly near 100%. That's a real limitation of using this scorecard alone. But it doesn't change the internal comparison that matters here: among Republican incumbents endorsed by the same PAC, funded recipients still score meaningfully lower than endorsed-only ones — a pattern that isn't explained away by partisan lean, since every name in that comparison is the same party.

So what does predict it?

Not a pro-education voting record, and not committee membership alone — three sitting Senate Education Committee members (Fillmore, Hinkins, McKell) get zero dollars. Laying every factor found across this page side by side, actual money tracks a different, narrower set of things than the broad endorsement list does.

What predicts an endorsement

All 42 names, low cost to grant
  • Being on the ballot in a legislative or State Board of Education race
  • No litmus test — genuinely bipartisan, includes sitting Democrats
  • No measurable correlation with UEA voting score (highest and lowest scorers both appear)
  • No measurable correlation with committee membership (some committee members endorsed, most aren't)

What predicts actual money

Only 8 of 42, a separately-targeted decision
  • Committee leadership, not just membership — Logan Monson is Vice Chair of House Health & Human Services; ordinary committee members get nothing
  • Top institutional position — Speaker Mike Schultz
  • A multi-year, repeat relationship — Jerry Stevenson (both entities, 2021-2022), Logan Monson (2023 and 2026), Jake Sawyer (both entities, multiple years)
  • Proximity to a specific high-value policy fight — Dave Shallenberger (SB284/Stratos), Jefferson Moss (Privacy Governing Board predecessor)
  • A genuinely competitive race — Jake Sawyer's HD9 seat also drew Meta's $98,942.90 same-day spend

Put plainly: one list is broad and costs nothing to extend across party lines; the money is narrow and goes to people who can move a bill, chair a vote, or decide a contested seat — regardless of how they've actually voted on the causes the PAC claims to care about. Is a broad endorsement list and a narrow, targeted set of checks two arms of the same strategy, or two different purposes sharing one name?

10. Worth Asking

  • An "education advocacy" organization whose registered agent, filer, and Executive Committee President all work for a private prison company. Does that change how a reader should weigh its self-description as a bipartisan, consensus-driven education group?
  • Two differently-branded giving entities sending an identical $2,500 to the same sitting MIDA Vice Chair, one week apart. Coincidence, or two channels used by the same small group of people to reach the same relationship without it showing up as one large check?
  • Utah Behavior Services gave $3,000 to House Speaker Mike Schultz. Schultz separately personally funded Preserving Utah Values PAC with $120,000 — a PAC that ran attack ads against Democratic candidates Angela Choberka, Scott Stephenson, and Rosemary Lesser in 2024 and 2026. UBS's money didn't go to that PAC directly, but it did go to the person who bankrolled it. Does giving money to a legislative leader carry any responsibility for what that leader does with his own resources afterward — or is that too many steps removed to matter?

12. The Parent Organization's Other PAC

While building this page, a records trail led to something bigger than a related organization: Education First Utah's own parent nonprofit — "Education First, A Utah Nonprofit Corporation" — also directly owns a second political giving vehicle, "Our Schools Now PAC." Same registered agent (Ammaron Wolfgramm), same 500 N Marketplace Drive address.

Our Schools Now was a real, high-profile 2018 Utah ballot campaign — also known as "Our Kids 1st" and "Utah Students for Question 1" — built to pass a citizen's ballot initiative raising taxes to fund Utah schools. Its own Articles of Incorporation state that purpose explicitly. It raised $2,029,434.54 in 2018 alone, with Gail Miller as its single largest donor at $250,000 — the same Gail Miller who chairs Education First Utah and tops CMV PAC's own donor list. Management & Training Corporation gave $101,046.32 directly. Its own officers — Scott Anderson (Chairman, Zions Bank), Robert Scott Marquardt (Secretary/Treasurer, MTC CEO), Nolan Karras, and Jesselie Anderson — are the identical four people who make up Education First Utah's Executive Committee.

Most tellingly: Our Schools Now's own official state filing lists "Education First" as its formally declared Affiliated Organization — not an inferred connection, a stated one, on the record with the Utah Lieutenant Governor's office.

The same parent entity behind a PAC that states on its own site it is "not advocating any tax increases" also directly owns a PAC built to pass one of the largest tax increases for education in recent Utah history. Full breakdown, funders, and officers on the dedicated case file.
