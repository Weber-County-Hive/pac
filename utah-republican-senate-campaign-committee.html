Utah Republican Senate Campaign Committee: $3.46 Million Out Since 2018, and the Senators It Funds

The PAC that represents the Utah Senate Republican Caucus reported about $3,458,307 in spending from 2018 through Sept. 25, 2026. It gives to Republican senators’ campaigns, holds donor events and golf tournaments, and pays its treasurer’s firm, Irish Elk LLC, which received at least $386,453.15 from 2021 through 2026. All figures come from the committee’s own filings with the state.

1. Start Here

IN PLAIN WORDSA caucus PAC raises money for a party’s members in one chamber of the Legislature. This one represents “Members of the Utah Senate Republican Caucus.” It is run by Senate President J. Stuart Adams and Sen. Kirk Cullimore, with consultant Kyle Palmer as treasurer. Most of its money comes from companies, trade groups and other PACs. It passes much of it to Republican senators’ campaigns and spends the rest on events, consultants and its treasurer’s firm. These arrangements are legal in Utah and are disclosed on its reports.

What the records show

  • Its treasurer’s firm is its largest payee. Irish Elk LLC was paid at least $386,453.15 from 2021 through Sept. 25, 2026. Before Irish Elk first appears in 2021, Kyle Palmer was paid $101,204.96 personally (2019–2021), listed as fundraising fees and PAC administration. In 2026 the committee also paid an “NCSL Travel Stipend - Kyle Palmer” of $3,448.79.
  • Every gift on its latest report came from a company, group or PAC. The 2026 September 30th Report lists 54 contributions totaling $188,820. None is from an individual person.
  • It gives to senators’ campaigns. From 2022 through May 21, 2026, the largest gifts went to Wayne Harper and Todd Weiler ($62,500 each), Brady Brammer and Dan McCay ($55,000 each), and John Johnson and Lincoln Fillmore ($52,500 each).
  • Events are a large share of spending. Wasatch Mountain State Park’s golf course and cafe received $124,926 from 2022 through May 2026, and Grand America Hotel $88,613.63. In 2026 the committee paid $40,862.64 for golf tournament bags and $19,921.38 for catering at Soldier Hollow.
  • $25,000 went to Committee to Elect Stuart Adams on June 23, 2026, primary day. The committee also gave his campaign $4,500 in 2021.
  • DCI Group was paid $68,000 for “Opinion Research” on July 31, 2026.

11. Graded by the Rubric

QuestionGradeWhy
PowerFOn the 2026 September 30th Report, all 54 contributions ($188,820) came from companies, trade groups and PACs; individual people gave $0 (0%). Fixed scale: under 10% = F. Earlier years were not split by category, so this grade uses the latest report, as the scale allows.
TransparencyCThe report was submitted on its due date, Sept. 30, and every contributor and payee is named. In the 2022–2026 export, the 29 Irish Elk payments are listed with the purpose “Other”; the September 30th Report adds descriptions (“Q3 PAC Administration,” “Fundraising Fees”). The public report shows the category; it may not show descriptions a filer types. That meets the law.
Financial accountabilityCPayments to the treasurer, his firm and an officer’s own campaign are itemized and dated, as Utah law requires. The record does not show what the Irish Elk payments listed as “Other” bought.
Environmental impactN/ANot part of this record.
Community impactN/ANot part of this record.
D+Overall: the average of F, C and C (1.33), between D and C.

How to read these: a C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the PAC went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened; F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. Power uses the Hive’s fixed money scale, the same for every committee and party: A = 90% or more of counted money from individual people; B = 67–89%; C = 34–66%; D = 10–33%; F = under 10%. Overall is the average of the graded rows (A = 4 … F = 0; N/A and dashes left out), with a plus or minus showing where it falls between letters. Grades judge the record, not the people, and change when the record changes.

Going beyond the law would look like: saying on each report what each payment to a consultant bought, and raising money from individual people as well as organizations.
