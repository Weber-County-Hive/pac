Adams Leadership PAC: $1.22 Million Out, and $258,103 to the Treasurer’s Firm

Senate President J. Stuart Adams’s leadership PAC reported $1,221,703.57 in spending from November 2019 through Sept. 25, 2026, $511,977.86 of it in 2026. Its treasurer, Kyle Palmer, also runs Irish Elk LLC, which the PAC paid $258,103.65 from 2021 through 2026. All figures come from the PAC’s own filings with the state.

1. Start Here

IN PLAIN WORDSA leadership PAC is a political committee a lawmaker sponsors. It collects money from companies, groups and people, and gives it to other campaigns or spends it on political work. This one is sponsored by Senate President J. Stuart Adams. Its treasurer, Kyle Palmer, is a consultant whose firm the PAC also pays. That arrangement is legal in Utah and is disclosed on the PAC’s reports.

$1,221,703.57Spent, Nov. 15, 2019 – Sept. 25, 2026
$511,977.86Spent in 2026 alone (through Sept. 25)
$258,103.65Paid to Irish Elk LLC, 2021–2026
$100,000To Committee to Elect Stuart Adams, 2026

What the records show

  • Spending grew in 2026. The PAC reported $69,552.09 out in 2021 and $511,977.86 in the first nine months of 2026, its largest year.
  • The treasurer’s firm is the PAC’s largest payee. Irish Elk LLC was paid $258,103.65 from 2021 through 2026. Kyle Palmer was also paid $17,837.47 personally in 2020 through 2024, listed as fundraising fees and similar items.
  • $100,000 went to Adams’s own campaign in 2026: $45,000 on Jan. 12 and $55,000 on July 22, a month after the June 23 primary.
  • Election Hive was paid $94,594.72 across the PAC’s life, including $68,000 during the 2026 primary.
  • The Utah Republican Party received $71,903.73, including $48,603.73 on May 20, 2026.
  • Travel: $86,181.27 to airlines and travel agencies from 2023 through 2025.
  • The account ended the Sept. 30th period with $622.43, after starting the period with $180,001.43.

A correction to an earlier tracker line. An older Hive research tracker listed “Adams PAC → House Speaker Leadership PAC, $68,000 (2026 Primary).” That payment does not appear in the PAC’s state records. The 2026 Primary report instead shows $58,000 and $10,000 to Election Hive, which total $68,000. This page uses the state records.

8. Graded by the Rubric

QuestionGradeWhy
PowerDOn the 2026 September 30th Report, individual people gave $4,500 of the $30,500 counted (14.8%). The rest came from companies, groups and committees. Fixed scale: 10–33% = D. Earlier years were not split by category, so this grade uses the latest report, as the scale allows.
TransparencyCThe report was submitted Sept. 25, before its Sept. 30 due date, and every contributor and payee is named. Most consulting lines show only the category “Other - Consulting.” The public report shows the category; it may not show descriptions a filer types. That meets the law.
Financial accountabilityCPayments to the treasurer’s own firm and to the sponsor’s own campaign are itemized and dated, as Utah law requires. The record does not show what work the consulting payments bought.
Environmental impactN/ANot part of this record.

C−Overall: the average of D, C and C (1.67), between D and C.

How to read these: a C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the PAC went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened; F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. Power uses the Hive’s fixed money scale, the same for every committee and party: A = 90% or more of counted money from individual people; B = 67–89%; C = 34–66%; D = 10–33%; F = under 10%. Overall is the average of the graded rows (A = 4 … F = 0; N/A and dashes left out), with a plus or minus showing where it falls between letters. Grades judge the record, not the people, and change when the record changes.

Going beyond the law would look like: saying on each report what each consulting payment bought, and raising most of the money from individual people.

9. Questions to Ask, and Who Can Answer

  • Ask the Lieutenant Governor’s elections office (disclosure@utah.gov, 801-538-1041):Does Utah have any rule on a PAC treasurer’s own business being paid by the PAC, and where is it written?
  • Ask your state senator or representative:Would you support requiring PAC reports to say what each consulting payment bought?
  • Ask the Lieutenant Governor’s elections office:What happens to a leadership PAC’s remaining money when its sponsor leaves office?
